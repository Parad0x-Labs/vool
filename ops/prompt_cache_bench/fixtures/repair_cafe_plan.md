# Riverside Repair Cafe: plan for the first season

## Why we are doing this
Our street throws away a surprising amount of things that are nearly working: lamps with a loose wire, trousers with a broken zip, bicycles with a slipped chain, toasters that only heat one side. Three neighbours (Ines Duarte, Kofi Mensah and Hanna Lind) agreed in August to run a monthly repair cafe where people bring broken items and volunteers help them fix them. The goal for the first season is simple: keep things out of the bin, teach people one repair skill each, and find out whether the street wants this every month.

## When and where
The cafe runs on the second Saturday of each month from October to March, six sessions in total. Doors open at 10:00 and the last item is accepted at 13:30; we close at 14:30. The venue is the back hall of the Riverside Community Centre, 14 Willow Lane. The hall holds 60 people standing or 40 seated. The centre charges 35 euros per session for the hall, which includes tables, chairs, heating and use of the small kitchen. The first session is on 10 October; the second on 14 November; the third on 12 December.

## Repair stations
We will run five stations, each with one lead volunteer:
1. Electrical (lamps, small appliances, chargers) - lead: Kofi. Rule: nothing that plugs into the wall is opened while plugged in, and every repaired appliance gets a visual check and a test on the RCD-protected extension before it goes home. No microwaves and no anything with a large capacitor.
2. Textiles (zips, hems, buttons, small tears) - lead: Hanna. Two sewing machines on loan from the school.
3. Bicycles (chains, brakes, punctures, gears) - lead: Marco Bellini. Works outdoors under the canopy if dry.
4. Furniture and wood (wobbly chairs, drawers, small joinery) - lead: Ines.
5. Electronics advice (phones, laptops) - lead: Priya Nair. Advice only in the first season: diagnosis, battery and screen part sourcing, no soldering.

Each station has a sign-in sheet. Visitors may bring at most two items per visit so the queue stays fair. Children are welcome but must stay with an adult at the electrical and bicycle stations.

## Budget for the season
Income: a grant of 900 euros from the municipal Small Green Projects fund, which has been confirmed, plus a donation jar. We will not charge an entry fee.
Planned costs:
- Hall hire: 6 sessions x 35 euros = 210 euros.
- Tools and consumables (solder is not needed this season; fuses, cable, thread, zips, inner tubes, glue, screws): 320 euros.
- Insurance (public liability for volunteer events, through the federation): 145 euros for the season.
- Printing posters and the feedback cards: 60 euros.
- Coffee, tea and biscuits: 15 euros per session, 90 euros total.
Total planned: 825 euros, leaving 75 euros of the grant as a reserve for broken tools.

## Safety rules
- Volunteers decide whether an item is safe to repair; they can refuse any item.
- Visitors do the repair themselves where possible, with the volunteer guiding; this is a learning space, not a free repair shop.
- A first aid kit is kept at the kitchen door and Hanna holds the first aid certificate.
- The fire exit at the back of the hall must stay clear; the bicycle station never blocks it.

## How we measure the season
At the sign-in table, every item gets a ticket with its station and outcome: fixed, partly fixed, not fixable, or advice given. We will count items per station and outcome after each session. The success line for the season: at least 120 items seen in total and at least half of them fixed or partly fixed. We will also ask every visitor one question on a feedback card: "Would you come again next month?"
